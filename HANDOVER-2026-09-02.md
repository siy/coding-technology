## The defect worth knowing about: one false claim, four surfaces

*"An operator's config change reaches a running slice"* stood simultaneously in
`aether-overview.md:386` (pragmatica), the Aether book's *Config inheritance* section, and two
`aether-coder` reference docs. **None was wrong when written**; each drifted separately, and
correcting one corrected none of the others. The book copy was found only because somebody
happened to be writing the adjacent section — luck, not process.

**Verified independently, twice:** `SpiResourceProvider` mutates `promiseCache` at exactly
`:119` (first provision) and `:153` (last release). No invalidation path on config change exists.
`ConfigNotificationManager.notifyChange` has no production callers (#381).

**The statable guarantee, now in the book:** a config value delivered through a provisioned
resource is read once, at provision time, and does not refresh on a consensus config update; a
slice sees new configuration when it is next reloaded. **This holds for `ConfigurationSection`
too**, not only for user-defined types.

Three surfaces are fixed here. `aether-overview.md:386` is pragmatica's and sits on #496.

## Corrections this session made to its own work

Recorded because the pattern is the point, not the embarrassment.

- **The published RPP article claimed novelty that prior art refuted.** RAND's Assumption-Based
  Planning (Dewar, 1987/1993) pointed exactly this discipline at plans. Corrected on all three
  surfaces; the article now credits ABP and Shakey's 1972 executor and narrows the claim to *a
  signpost that reads itself*.
- **The Provisioned Step's precedent was narrower than first written.** `MethodInterceptor` is a
  *platform* type on a *platform* factory registered at node boot — the path user types could not
  use until `8d36f0c1c` (#773, closed 2026-09-01). Any example written before that commit would
  have failed at deployment with `ResourceFactoryNotFound`.
- **"No lifecycle today" was false.** Lifecycle exists; close fires only on last release, only
  for `AutoCloseable`, and **close failures are absorbed**.
- **An instance was inverted.** Resource lifecycle was written as adjacency-read-as-inheritance;
  it is the mirror image, *silence read as absence of behaviour*. That inversion produced the
  better rule: "state what you do not guarantee" is only half of it.

## Working notes

- **Verify peers rather than relay them.** Two of this session's corrections came from checking a
  peer's account before printing it. The argument that settled it: *a book claim outlives every
  session that could correct it*, so a peer's word is structurally insufficient there regardless
  of how reliable the peer is. The CTO extended it to anything published beyond the reach of the
  people who could correct it — a tagged release included.
- **Attribution has a ceiling.** Relaying "X says Y" moves a claim from unverifiable-and-
  unattributed to unverifiable-and-attributed, and no further. I relayed a role to the CTO without
  attributing it and they correctly refused to act on it.
- **Rule per-ticket, shape in the skill.** Operational instructions belong in the first screen of
  the ticket that needs them; the general shape belongs in the skill. Mixing them makes the rule
  read as commentary.
