## 1 · Commitments as they stood 2010–2012 (hindsight-clean)

- **White Paper: "Universal Credit: welfare that works" (Cm 7957, Nov 2010).** PRIMARY. Full PDF: assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a79581640f0b63d72fc4fd9/universal-credit-full-document.pdf. Launch speech (gov.uk/government/speeches/universal-credit-welfare-that-works): "We expect to reduce administrative costs by more than half a billion pounds a year, and to reduce levels of fraud and error by £1 billion a year." Baseline: fraud+error "costing the taxpayer around £5.2bn a year." Mechanism claim: "by utilising the best data technology available, we will streamline the system to reduce administration costs and minimise opportunities for fraud and error at the same time."
- **Programme budget ~£2.4bn**: PRIMARY — VERIFIED verbatim, NAO, *Universal Credit: early progress* (HC 621, Session 2013-14, 5 Sept 2013), fetched and extracted from nao.org.uk PDF. Key facts, p.4: **"£2.4bn — expected cost of implementing Universal Credit, to 2023."** Summary §4, p.5: **"The Department plans to spend £2.4 billion to implement Universal Credit up to April 2023."** Part One §1.11, p.15, ties it to the business case directly: **"In its most recent business case, in December 2012, the Department planned to spend £2.4 billion in investment costs to introduce Universal Credit up to 2022-23. Investment costs include IT, support for claimants migrating on to Universal Credit, staff training, and estates costs."** Chapter wording ("a cost envelope of roughly £2.4 billion") matches; no correction needed.
- **Volume projection**: 7m+ households at full scale (NAO 2018 retrospective states original forecast "exceeding 7m households"). SECONDARY.
- **Completion target 2017**, national rollout from Oct 2013: PRIMARY-adjacent (gov.uk pathfinder-update news + NAO 2013).
- **Continuity constraint**: implicit-but-absolute in every planning document (existing benefits keep paying through transition); made explicit in the twin-track design and in Zora's 2026 language ("maintaining continuity of service"). Treat as PRIMARY by demonstration across all three cycles.
- **Digital by default** mandate: PRIMARY (White Paper); eight public computers at the first jobcentre + 130 in Tameside libraries (SECONDARY) show the demand met reality immediately.
- **Policy volatility**: six benefits merged, each with live amendment history; Welfare Reform Act 2012 + the 2012–2015 regulations series (gov.uk lists them). PRIMARY at the structural level; no single "amendment frequency" statistic — the case argues it from the regulations record, labeled.

## 2 · V₀ and the reset (2011–2013)

- **Spend**: £638m on UC IT by end-2012, of which £441m design and development (Computer Weekly). SECONDARY. £425m spent by mid-2013 with "over 70 per cent... on IT systems" (NAO 2013, HC 621 — PRIMARY, fetched earlier this session). Write-offs: £34m (NAO 2013); £40.1m figure also reported (Computer Weekly "DWP writes off £40m"). Up to "£130m of IT work would be scrapped" (NAO via Computer Weekly). SECONDARY.
- **Contracts**: £1.12bn let across HP, Accenture, Capgemini, IBM (Computer Weekly, quoting a former project consultant). Ceilings: IBM £525m, Accenture ~£500m, HP £100m; actual spend to March 2013: Accenture £125m, IBM £75m, HP £49m, BT £16m (NAO 2013 / Computer Weekly cross-check, verified 2026-07-11). IT costs £360m to Oct 2013, HP largest at £197m. SECONDARY-strong.
- **The load-bearing quote (boundaries-predate-reasons):** former agile consultant on the project: **"We were effectively on a waterfall project, because it was a waterfall contract."** (Computer Weekly, "Why agile development failed for Universal Credit"). SECONDARY.
- **"Not fit for purpose":** Norma Wood (Cabinet Office Major Projects Authority) to the Public Accounts Committee, Sept 2013 — much of the existing UC IT "not fit for purpose" and unlikely to be reusable. SECONDARY (PAC hearing via Computer Weekly).
- **Reset reviews**: January 2013 Capgemini review; April 2013 "Reset IT stocktake" concluding "limited extensibility," no blueprint/target architecture/operating model (NAO 2013 narrative). PRIMARY-adjacent.
- **IBM Cúram as V₀'s core product: UNVERIFIED.** Targeted search found no primary or strong secondary source naming Cúram in UC's build. The IBM contract (£525m ceiling / £75m-per-year over 60 applications incl. customer information, fraud referral) is documented; the specific product is not. **Ruling for prose: do not assert; the section stands on the waterfall-contract structure, the not-fit-for-purpose finding, and the write-offs without it.** (The case file's [RECONSTRUCTION] on entity-model coupling must argue from the *contract and finding record*, not from a product name.)
- **Suppliers frozen out of the rebuild**: draft business case documents, late 2013 — HP, Accenture, IBM, BT to have "significantly less" involvement in the digital solution (Computer Weekly). SECONDARY.
- **Identity-assurance requirement straining (2013)**: PRIMARY — VERIFIED verbatim, NAO HC 621 (as above), Part Three §§3.18-3.20, p.34. §3.18: **"The Department was unclear about how far its security solution depended on the IRIS 'trust' component, which assesses and establishes confidence in a user's identity. The Department did not know what would be required to make it work in combination with other security components... The pathfinder uses an interim solution because the Department has delayed development of a strategic risk and trust system."** §3.19: **"The Department is also reconsidering its plans for identity assurance (IDA) of online users. It originally planned to develop an IDA solution for Universal Credit and Personal Independence Payment, which would ultimately form the basis of a cross-government approach. In December 2011, the Cabinet Office decided that the proposed solution was too expensive and unfit for cross-government purposes. During 2012, the Department continued with developing its own solution but there were delays in securing funding and finalising the tender for IDA providers."** §3.20: **"In January 2013, the Cabinet Office took responsibility for several strands of the cross-government IDA platform from the Department. The Department and the Cabinet Office IDA Programme Team will resume work later in 2013..."** Chapter wording ("a security and identity-assurance requirement the 2013 record already shows straining") is an accurate, appropriately hedged paraphrase; no correction needed.

## 3 · The twin-track path (2013–2019) + grade + Zora (2026)

- **Pathfinder**: 29 April 2013, Ashton-under-Lyne Jobcentre first (Welfare Reform Act 2012 Commencement Order No. 9); Wigan/Warrington/Oldham staged behind it (claims from 1 and 29 July); **deliberately narrow: straightforward single unemployed claimants, ~1,500/month cap**; IDS framing "careful and controlled." PRIMARY (gov.uk news + commencement order) + SECONDARY detail.
- **Rollout**: full-service national completion Dec 2018; live service closed to new claims 2018; live service decommissioned 2019 (BROWNFIELD-CANDIDATES, NAO-derived). SECONDARY-strong.
- **NAO 2018 (Rolling Out Universal Credit, HC 1123, Session 2017-2019, 15 June 2018)**: PRIMARY — VERIFIED verbatim, fetched and extracted from nao.org.uk PDF. Summary §7, p.7: **"It has spent £1.3 billion of its investment so far on creating Universal Credit, and £600 million on running costs. About 10% (815,000) of the eventual number of claimants are now claiming Universal Credit."** Part One §1.20, p.19: **"In March 2018, 815,000 claimants received Universal Credit. This is about 10% of the total caseload expected when all claimants are migrated from legacy benefits."** Original 7m+ forecast, Figure 5 note 1, p.26: **"The original forecast was that more than seven million households would be on Universal Credit by now, while the latest figures show that 660,000 households were on Universal Credit in December 2017."** (Note: this figure is *households* in Dec 2017, distinct from the 815,000 *claimants* in March 2018 quoted above — both trace to the same NAO report but are not interchangeable.) Hardship finding, Summary §10, p.7: **"We spoke to local and national bodies that, together, work with a significant minority of claimants. They showed us evidence that many of these people have suffered difficulties and hardship during the rollout of the full service. These have resulted from a combination of issues with the design of Universal Credit and its implementation."** — **flag for prose**: the chapter's line ("the 2018 NAO report found rollout pace imposing hardship on claimants") narrows causation to *pace* specifically; the source attributes hardship to "a combination of issues with the design of Universal Credit and its implementation," of which pace/dual-state duration is one plausible reading but not the NAO's own framing. Consider softening to "found hardship among claimants during rollout" or explicitly marking the pace-attribution as the chapter's own inference.
- **NAO Feb 2024**: 6.3m claimants (Dec 2023); completion 6+ years late vs 2018 business case; managed migration: 1 in 5 invited tax-credit claimants did not move and had benefits stopped. SECONDARY.
- **COVID surge (the V₁ grade)**: DWP Digital blog, 14 Dec 2020 (PRIMARY, fetched earlier this session): 2m pre-COVID users → 5m active claims at peak; 950k applications in a fortnight; 2.2m calls/day peak; release cadence 6 → 76 urgent changes in 2 months; scaled without legislative-deadline changes.
- **Fraud/error outcome vs the 2010 target**: later NAO assessment: 9.4% (£1.7bn) of payments vs 6.4% forecast — actual ran significantly above the White Paper expectation. SECONDARY. (Grade-side fact: the *architecture* absorbed COVID; the *business-case* fraud number did not land — the case must keep these two grades distinct.)
- **Project Zora**: PIN notice April 2026, bidstats.uk/tenders/2026/W16/868745235 — title verbatim: "UC Project Zora - Application Decomposition & Microservices Transition"; addresses "the constraints of a long-standing monolithic application estate"; will "progressively decompose elements of the Universal Credit application into more modular, loosely coupled components aligned to a modern, microservices-based architecture... while **maintaining continuity of service**." PublicTechnology, 4 June 2026: £40m plan, market-input phase, provider appointment to follow. PRIMARY (PIN) + SECONDARY (press). Note for the case: Zora is itself a *procurement* seeking an external provider — a structural echo of 2011 worth one neutral sentence.
