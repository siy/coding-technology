## 1 · The judgment inventory (what the derivation hands back)

Each item states *what the mechanics produce* vs *what a human decides* — the methodology-informs-business-decides pattern, instantiated:

- **Recovery-class weighing.** When domain shape admits more than one class (an inverse exists AND the state tolerates decay), the ledger prices both; **the pick is a business preference** (restored consistency vs liveness — money vs motion). The derivation's output is the priced pair, not the answer. (F9's corollary: when the inverse must be *designed*, compensation is itself a use case with SLOs — someone decides it's worth building.)
- **The renegotiation menu (ch. 8's handback).** A contradiction's menu is derived and priced; choosing which commitment bends is the business call by definition — the commitments were theirs.
- **The answers themselves.** The sheet elicits and prices; the business sets every number. The derivation owns *consequences*, never *targets*. ("An SLO you haven't priced is a wish" cuts both ways: pricing is the methodology's job, wishing is legitimately the business's.)
- **The conscious second driver.** Multi-driver cohesion is permitted but must be *named* (the premium-preemption edge) — the naming is a judgment that a boundary is worth blurring.
- **Enterprise-altitude inputs.** Vision, org strategy, market bets enter as answers (release structure, cost envelope, multi-X); the book takes them as inputs honestly rather than faking a model of them.

The chapter's stance sentence: *the derivation doesn't shrink judgment; it relocates judgment to where it is genuinely owed — and strips it from everywhere it was just taste with tenure.*

## 2 · The MTTR/MTBF dissolution (the worked example of the boundary)

The arc (corpus + citations in NOTES-mttr-mtbf.md): the trend is neither joined nor fought — it is dissolved per failure class, and the dissolution demonstrates section 1 in action.

1. **The slogan as received** — Vogels ambient ("everything fails all the time" as epigraph candidate), Allspaw's title as everyone read it.
2. **The dropped qualifier** — the scope was *in the title* ("for most types of F"); accidental data loss named as the exception in the origin post itself. The boundary the recovery triple derives was there in 2010; the slogan erased its own scope.
3. **The per-class refinement** — BER/FER/design-out: repair-first is FER's stance, prevent-first is BER's price, and **design-out transcends the dichotomy — the failure class stops existing, and both metrics go undefined on it.** Jeep vs Rolls-Royce lands here: repairability is a purchased capability with a mechanism, per component — a ledger row, not a philosophy.
4. **The metric's death** — Allspaw's 2017 self-retraction (the *mean* is the error), VOID's skew data, Davidovič's Monte Carlo: two independent kills. Unified with ch. 5: **means lie about incident durations for the same reason they lie about latency — scalars hide scope and shape.** One discipline, two chapters.
5. **What remains standing** — priced targets per operation (Google CRE error budgets = Q2 in Google's voice; "as available as is required, but not much more" = the selection rule independently converged); MTBF/MTTR demoted to *derived planning levers* under a priced target (the CRE arithmetic, ch. 5 seam); verification by injection (GameDay = the recovery axis's consistency-lens, exercised).

Close of the section: *the trend, derived instead of debated* — and the judgment that remains (which failure classes get which class of recovery at what price) is exactly section 1's first item.
