## Read this first: three things that will bite a successor

**1. The one durable output of these four days is a verification rule, and it is not "be careful".**

> A check can only speak about the space it can reach, and neither a clean result nor a passing control
> tells you what that space was.

Two rules follow that cost something: **state the space searched, not only the result** ("no `-T` in
`.github/workflows/`" is checkable by anyone; "CI runs sequentially" is not), and **this class is caught
by a second reader of the artifact, never by re-running the search.** It is in the workspace stance with
six worked instances. Three were mine, including one committed **hours after I wrote the rule**: I
filtered sentences to those containing a digit and a claim word, so a disproving sentence with no digit
could never be returned, and I reported its absence as fact. Assume this applies to your own checks.

**2. Two board items are live and the decisions are the owner's, not in this repo.** Everything is in
private `oss`, pushed. Do not re-derive any of it from here.

**3. This tree was found on another session's branch mid-work.** An agent from the CTO session was
launched into `coding-technology`, branched, committed, and left HEAD on `docs/books-teaching-order-c`.
Uncommitted work survived the switch, nothing was lost, but a commit would have landed on the wrong
branch. **Run `git rev-parse --abbrev-ref HEAD` at session start.** Do not trust that the tree is on
`main` because you left it there.

## The article, because five of its claims were wrong and a reader caught them

A reviewing agent graded the article's claims. **Five were real overclaims and are now fixed**: a
complicated leaf is *always* a decomposition failure (genuinely hard algorithms exist); *everything*
that required thought is in a leaf (deriving the graph is thought); leaf logging is *sufficient*
instrumentation (it is complete *effect* coverage, a smaller claim); debt is *cheap to clear* and *a
flow not a stock* (visible and located hold, cheap does not); and losing the item boundary makes the
graph *cyclic* (it stops being the right model, which is not the same).

The same review supplied ~150 further properties. **Most were already in the book** — location encoding
coupling radius, recursive closure, consumer-owned step interfaces, duplication being correct under
different change drivers. I recommended extracting the strongest one and then found it already written,
better, at `book/project-structure.md:164`. That is the finding: **the comparison document's value is
the contrast, not properties we lack**, which is the strongest argument for keeping it out of the book.
