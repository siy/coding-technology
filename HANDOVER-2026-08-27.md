## The saga ruling — settled, and better than the options put up

Aether's #354 ships a `Saga<C>` facade, which collided with two published articles arguing the saga
is not a pattern and is an antipattern across services. Three options were put to Sergiy (keep and
scope it, rename to the book's vocabulary, or soften the book). **He took none of them.** His
ruling: state the correspondence in the book between *compensated sequence* and *saga*, then use the
two interchangeably.

That dissolves the tension instead of managing it. A stated synonym is not a competing primitive, so
`architecture-synthesis.md:225` keeps "The saga is not a primitive" verbatim and gains the composite's
name; the glossary gains a **Compensated sequence** entry. **Aether proceeds with `Saga<C>` as
specified — no rename, no alias, no doc caveat.**

The CTO recorded the ruling on #354 itself so stream B inherits it from the ticket. It also pinned a
wording guide worth keeping: **a durable ledger buys crash-safety, not transactionality.** The book
defines the mechanism as BER across autonomous steps *that share no transactional substrate*, and
`Saga<C>` over `DurableEntity` fits that cleanly — a ledger is what you need *because* the steps are
not transactional. The definition licenses the facade rather than straining against it.

Both arms converged perfectly, **on different graphs**. All five control implementations gather the

**Two limitations bound the headline and are stated as limitations, not escapes.** The metric

**What it licenses:** determinism is **not distinctive** — convergence was achieved without the
methodology. **What it does not license:** any claim that JBCT lacks determinism; both arms were
perfectly deterministic.

```

cp target/jbct.jar ~/.jbct/lib/jbct.jar
cd ~/IdeaProjects/pragmatica
git worktree remove /tmp/pragmatica-toolchain
```
