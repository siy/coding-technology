## The mutation research, and what it actually concluded

Ran PIT over `jbct-loan/jbct-slice`, `ticketing`, and `jbct-loan/spring-boot` (non-JBCT control).
Full numbers and reproduction recipe in `../oss/tmp/mutation-findings.md`; pre-refactor baseline in
`../oss/tmp/mutation-baseline-pre-refactor.md`.

**What went into the book (4.7.0):** 441 logic mutants across two JBCT codebases, **none in a
composition**. That gives the 4.6.0 four-facts rule a *mechanism* rather than an argument — the
four facts are a boundary, not a budget, because they are the whole of what a composition can do.
Mutation testing is named as the exercised-vs-discharged diagnostic, explicitly **not a target**,
with three limits stated: two thirds of raw mutants are null-return noise on JBCT code, predicates
delegated to a library are invisible to the tool, and *a near-empty report is a question, not a
result*.

**What did not go into the book, and must not without new evidence:**

- *"94% of decidable surface sits at the leaf"* — describes `jbct-loan`'s idiom, not JBCT.
- Any mutation **score** comparison — suites differ 5x in size and in vintage.
- Anything about Spring. The control was written by JBCT's author as a comparison; a control group
  built to lose proves little, and the finding is now modest enough that publishing it would be
  *more* tempting and no more sound.

**The finding worth developing (article material, not book):** business logic is conserved across
technology. 41% of decision-bearing method names are shared verbatim between the functional and
imperative implementations; eight have identical branch counts. Spring's `scoreAdjustment` is one
method with 37 branches; JBCT's is six named methods totalling 35 — same logic, six independently
reachable test targets versus one. Neither paradigm reduces the domain; the methodology relocates
6% of the surface out of the orchestrator and decomposes what remains. That framing is publishable
precisely because it concedes the large number.

This is the naming census's result at a second level: that found domain vocabulary irreducible at
68.5%; this finds the *logic* irreducible by an independent instrument. **All three measurements
this fortnight found the mechanical fraction smaller than the books implied and the domain
fraction larger.** That is a positioning finding, not a defect — the methodology's value is
placement and attribution, not the elimination of complexity.

## Mistakes

**1. I classified mutants by class name, twice, and it reversed two conclusions.** `ticketing`
nests its leaves inside the slice-implementation record, so `eligibilityGate` carries a
`BuyTicket$1buyTicket` class name; I filed it as a composition and concluded `ticketing` inverted
the distribution, which killed a real finding. Separately I read Spring's `.service.` package as
"composition equivalent" and inflated a difference sevenfold. Both dissolved under **method-level**
classification. Neither was caught by any check — both were caught by the user asking *"routing has
no logic, so how can mutants be in routing?"*
**Rule: classify a mutant by its containing method, never its class.**

**2. `grep 'if ('` as a proxy for decision density.** Predicted JBCT would have half Spring's
conditionals; it has more (497 vs 365 logic mutants). JBCT puts decisions in *expressions* —
`value >= 1 && value <= 100` is two conditional jumps and zero `if (` matches. Third grep-proxy
error of the session, same shape each time: a text search cannot answer a structural question.

**3. Four failed PIT setup attempts** before a run succeeded: `-o` blocked the plugin download,
invented plugin versions (1.20.4/1.2.4 do not exist; 1.19.1/1.2.2 do), no compiled classes because
the copy excluded `target/`, and Java 25 bytecode unreadable by PIT (`class file major version 69`
— compile at release 24).

**4. A near-miss on the changelog.** While writing the 4.7.0 entry I anchored on `### Added` and
re-emitted `### Changed`, stranding the pre-existing entries under the wrong heading. Caught within
seconds by the header check, reverted, redone. Same shape as the 4.4.0 deletion two days ago; the
difference was checking immediately rather than two days later. **Diff the tail against `HEAD`
before committing that file** — it is now the routine and it works.

## Conventions confirmed

- **Changelogs are mechanical.** A list of what changed and where. Argument, provenance, and scope
  caveats belong in the book. Saved to memory as `feedback_changelog_mechanical`.
- **Classify by method, not by class.** Class names encode packaging conventions that differ between
  codebases following the same methodology.
- **A text search cannot answer a structural question.** Three failures this session from one habit.
- **Measure a second corpus before writing.** One codebase produced a confident, wrong paragraph
  that was two hours from shipping; the second sample cost an hour and prevented it.
- **Diff the tail against `HEAD` before committing a changelog.** Caught one corruption two days
  late and one within seconds.
- **A control group built to lose proves little** — and the more plausible the finding, the more
  careful to state what the control cannot support.
