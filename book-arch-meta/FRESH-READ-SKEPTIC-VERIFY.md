## Cross-cutting NEW-PROBLEM: the debt/unforced-position dichotomy now defends the founding thesis itself

Three separate fixes (F2, F3, F18) all resolve the "rival causal account" objection the same way: any architectural boundary not traceable to a priced answer is relabeled **unforced** / **debt**, regardless of who or what actually drew it — taste, org chart, or a four-vendor waterfall contract. Before 0.3.7 this move existed narrowly, scoped to Chapter 9's audit mechanism. It now does heavier work: it is the book's answer to "what if contracts/org-structure determine architecture, not derivation" — three times, in three chapters, verbatim-similar phrasing ("the org chart that overrode the derivation left debt behind, not a new kind of structure" / "the contract did not disprove the derivation. It stood in for one").

This is a real improvement over silence, and it is internally consistent. But it has the same shape as F10's original problem (the Companies House topology-miss absorption) before that finding got a pre-registered falsifier: **any outcome is compatible with "derived, not chosen," because any counter-instance is, by definition, "debt."** Unlike F10, this version of the move has *not* been given a parallel falsifier — the book never says what observation, short of nothing, would count against "boundaries not forced by answers are always attributable to unpriced inputs or debt, never to an independent determinant of architecture." Companies House's topology got a named disconfirmation condition; the founding thesis's rival-causation defense did not receive the same treatment, despite being asked to do more work in 0.3.7 than in 0.3.6. This is the single most important open question this round of edits creates rather than closes.

## Overall verdict

Nine of the ten changed passages you asked me to check land CLOSED or IMPROVED, and several are not cosmetic: the Companies House falsifiability fix (F10), the parametric-memory disclosure (F12), the in-book audit worked example (F17), and the misses-split (F9) are genuine methodological strengthening, argued and checked, not just reworded. Two findings (F1, F19) are untouched by explicit design choice and remain open exactly as filed. One (F13) got a partial, insufficient mitigation. The book is measurably more honest and more falsifiable than 0.3.6 on its evidentiary chapters.

But the founding-move fix (F2) and its extension to brownfield.md (F18) trade a *silent* gap for an *unfalsifiable* one: rival causal accounts are now named rather than ignored, but dispatched by a debt/input dichotomy that cannot lose. That dichotomy was sound and narrow when it lived only in Chapter 9's audit; stretched across three chapters to defend the book's central claim, it starts to look like the same move that the book itself, elsewhere, correctly identifies as a red flag (an absorbing category with no named disconfirmation condition). Until "derived, not chosen" gets a rival-causation falsifier as concrete as the one Companies House's topology miss now has, "derived, not chosen" holds at the precision the book claims for its *toolkit* — but not yet for its *thesis*.
