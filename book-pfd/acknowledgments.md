# Acknowledgments

Process-First Design is built on its own ground, but it did not arrive in an empty field. Where the work below reached the same territory, or named a thing the book needed named, the convergence is worth crediting.

**William Jackson** named the *semantic potential* of types — the idea that a type can carry a business statement and not merely a shape — in response to the author's *When Types Become the Business Language*. The phrase is his, and the book keeps it.

**Alexis King** gave *parse, don't validate* its name and its sharpest statement. The discipline runs through every step boundary in the spiral: input becomes a type whose existence is the proof that the data is good, and illegal states are made unrepresentable rather than checked for.

**Yannick Loth** formalized the change-driver criterion as the Independent Variation Principle. Process-First Design reaches the same partition from the process side and recognizes the Principle as independent corroboration rather than as a foundation it stands on — two paths to one territory, and the book is the better for the company. His later *On the Nature of Cohesion* goes further, giving cohesion itself a two-axis account, purity and completeness, that the book's recognition test now uses directly.

**Denys Poltorak** assembled *Architectural Metapatterns* (CC BY 4.0) and argued the completeness of its structures and transitions on geometric grounds. The book recognizes the catalog as corroboration for its deployment axis and its account of how inherited systems move from one shape to the next.

The wider convergence the book rests on — the practitioners who arrived at process-first design independently — is surveyed in the author's *The Quiet Consensus*. Full bibliographic detail for the works cited here is in the [References](references.md).
