# Blind Derivation — Companies House: Answer Sheet (demand side only)

## System context (Phase-1 facts)

Companies House is the executive agency of the Dept for Business & Trade that operates the statutory Registrar of Companies function under the Companies Act 2006, with separate Registrars for England & Wales (Cardiff), Scotland (Edinburgh) and Northern Ireland (Belfast), plus the Register of Overseas Entities (ROE) under the Economic Crime (Transparency and Enforcement) Act 2022. Core business operations: incorporate companies; accept ongoing statutory filings (confirmation statements, annual accounts, officer/PSC/address changes); maintain the register as the public legal record; provide free public search; publish bulk data products; dissolve/strike off and restore companies; since 4 March 2024, query, reject or remove submitted information and verify individuals' identities (Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023, "ECCTA").

Scale, FY2024/25 (FY2023/24 in parentheses), all from the annual report and accounts: UK register size 5.43m companies (5.35m); total filings accepted 14.7m (14.3m), 91.5% digital (92%); "Number of times the register was accessed: 16.3 billion (2023 to 2024: 16 billion)"; incorporations 801,871 (890,684); dissolutions 726,813 (663,167); companies in liquidation 112,418; ROE entities 32,054 (30,776). Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/companies-house-annual-report-and-accounts-2024-to-2025.

## The nine questions

**1. Time budget** — Confirmation statement: "Every company must, before the end of the period of 14 days after the end of each review period, deliver to the registrar" the statement (review period = 12 months from incorporation, then rolling 12-month periods). Companies Act 2006 s.853A, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/853A. Accounts: late-filing penalty bands stated as "Up to 1 month — £150", "1 to 3 months — £375", "3 to 6 months — £750", "More than 6 months — £1,500", doubled "if your accounts are late 2 years in a row." https://www.gov.uk/annual-accounts/penalties-for-late-filing. Incorporation: "Your company is usually registered within 24 hours" online; "Postal applications take 8 to 10 days." https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/register-your-company. Confirmation-statement enforcement (ECCTA): a warning notice gives "at least 28 days" before a £250–£2,000 penalty is issued. FOI: statutory 20-working-day deadline; 2024/25 actuals "832 FOI requests this year. 755 requests were responded to within the 20-day deadline" (annual report, above). Contact centre target: "answered within an average of 4 minutes" (actual 1m24s, annual report). UNKNOWN: no statutory turnaround time published for registrar query/reject decisions themselves.

**2. Failure budget** — Public target: "Digital services available for a minimum of 99.5% of the time"; actuals "Service availability: 99.98% (2023 to 2024: 100%)" (annual report, above). No per-operation criticality tiering was found in the public record — UNKNOWN whether e.g. incorporation is treated as more/less critical than search. A March 2026 WebFiling security issue closed the filing service for several days and was reported to the ICO and NCSC; a further closure of several online filing services occurred in June 2026, with Companies House confirming resulting missed deadlines "would generate late filing penalties, but they can be appealed" (ICAEW, https://www.icaew.com/insights/viewpoints-on-the-news/2026/jun-2026/companies-house-delays-with-processing-filings).

**3. Loss budget** — Dissolved company records: "retained for 20 years" then transferred to The National Archives or destroyed, per Companies Act 2006 s.1084; as of May 2026 Companies House "is reviewing the retention period" and "has paused the destruction and transfer of records during this review." https://www.gov.uk/government/news/companies-house-is-reviewing-the-retention-period-for-dissolved-company-records. Live-company records: retained indefinitely while active (implicit — register is the permanent statutory record). No explicit RPO/backup figure is published — UNKNOWN.

**4. Consistency contract** — The register is the authoritative legal record; the registrar "does not verify information for accuracy" and accepts filings "in good faith with only limited examination checks" (ICO decision notice context, https://ico.org.uk/media2/migrated/decision-notices/2258624/fs50711110.pdf). Correcting an error normally does not overwrite history: "the original incorrect form will remain on the register and cannot be removed" via the RP04 second-filing route; full removal requires a court order under CA2006 ss.1096–1097, "somewhere between 2–3 months" from instruction to rectification (https://mercantilebarristers.com/companies-house-rectification-claims/). A restored company "is deemed to have continued in existence as if it had not been struck off and dissolved" (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/restoring-a-company-to-the-companies-house-register/restoring-a-company-to-the-companies-house-register) — a retroactive legal-consistency fiction, not a technical guarantee. UNKNOWN: no public statement on acceptable staleness between an accepted filing and its visibility in public search.

**5. Load** — 14.7m filings/yr against 16.3bn register accesses/yr — Companies House separately states its search service is "satisfying over 12 billion searches a year" (annual report page) — a read:write ratio on the order of 1,000:1. December is the stated accounts-filing peak: "it's a busy time at Companies House because many companies choose to align their accounting period with the financial year, ending 31 March," making 31 December the modal accounts deadline (https://companieshouse.blog.gov.uk/2021/09/07/when-and-how-to-file-your-annual-accounts-with-companies-house/); in 2017 "503 people... filed within the hour before the deadline" while "28,874 missed the cut-off point completely" (same source). Confirmation statements are not calendar-concentrated — deadlines are spread across the year by incorporation anniversary (CA2006 s.853A, above). UNKNOWN: no published intraday/incorporation-day concentration figures.

**6. External constraints** — Statutory publication duty: the register "must" be made available under Companies Act 2006 Part 35, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/part/35/crossheading/inspection-etc-of-the-register. Overseas data transfer: UK GDPR Art.49(1)(g) permits transfers from "a register intended to provide information to the public" without an adequacy assessment; Data Protection Act 2018 Sch.2 Part 1 para.5 exempts Companies House from erasure duties for data "the controller is legally obliged to make available." Since ECCTA, "the registrar must not make available for public inspection any document required to contain relevant date of birth information" (legislation.gov.uk, above). FOI: statutory 20-working-day response duty (Freedom of Information Act; performance quoted in Q1). UNKNOWN: the public record search did not surface a specific Companies House statement on data-residency relocation triggered by the UK's EU exit — only the general public-register transfer exemption above; this should be marked UNKNOWN rather than inferred.

**7. Release structure** — Almost entirely UNKNOWN from the demand side; the only public cadence signal is legislative: ECCTA implementation runs through "around 50 statutory instruments," with "more than 30... made" by the relevant progress report (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/economic-crime-and-corporate-transparency-act-outline-transition-plan-for-companies-house), and dated obligations — mandatory identity verification "from 18 November 2025," corporate-director restrictions, mandatory iXBRL accounts "from 1 April 2027." These are legal change-obligation dates, not release cadence.

**8. Cost & capacity envelope** — FY2024/25 vs FY2023/24 (annual report, above): income "£220.3 million... compared to £90.5 million"; operating expenditure "£197.9 million (compared to £120.8 million"; staff costs "increased by £14 million to £88.8 million" (from £74.8m); headcount "rose to 1,866 by 31 March 2025 (from 1,397 as at 31 March 2024)"; average staff "1,635... as of 31 March 2025." Fees are set "on a cost recovery basis" (multiple fee-change sources, e.g. https://www.icaew.com/insights/viewpoints-on-the-news/2024/may-2024/new-companies-house-fees-bring-jump-in-costs-for-services); a 4% efficiency target was set and "4.1%" delivered (annual report). Funding also came via "the Economic Crime (anti-money laundering) Levy for a 3-year period ending March 2026" (Companies House business plan 2024–2025, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/companies-house-business-plan-2024-to-2025). No dedicated NAO value-for-money report on Companies House was located in permitted venues (only a Dept for Business & Trade departmental overview referencing it as a partner body) — marked UNKNOWN.

**9. Multi-X** — Three legal jurisdictions/registrars with distinct company-number prefixes (none/"SC"/"NI"; LLP prefixes OC/SO/NO) but one amalgamated public search (https://www.simpleformations.com/blog/why-are-uk-companies-registered-in-different-countries.html corroborated by CA2006 structure). Four register types in scope: companies, LLPs, limited partnerships (registered, not incorporated — "certificates of registration" not "certificates of incorporation," under the Limited Partnerships Act 1907), and the Register of Overseas Entities. New actor class: Authorised Corporate Service Providers, "over 1,000... registrations by 31 March 2025" (annual report). Identity verification applies to "more than 7 million individuals" (https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/09/The-Economic-Crime-and-Corporate-Transparency-Act-2023-Identity-Verification).

## Domain-shape facts

- **Incorporation has no simple inverse.** A company is dissolved/struck off (voluntary or registrar-initiated), not "deleted." Restoration is administrative (registrar, within 6 years, non-court-ordered strike-offs) or by court order (generally within 6 years, no time limit for personal-injury claims); a restored company "is deemed to have continued in existence" — a legal backfill, not a data operation. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/restoring-a-company-to-the-companies-house-register.
- **Filings are not directly editable.** Ordinary correction (Form RP04) layers a new filing; "the original incorrect form will remain on the register." True removal needs a court order under CA2006 ss.1096–1097 and is scoped to material that is legally invalid or factually inaccurate, weighed against third-party interest in the information staying public. https://mercantilebarristers.com/companies-house-rectification-claims/.
- **ECCTA gives the registrar a new, non-judicial inverse for a subset of cases.** The registrar can now query, reject before acceptance, or remove after acceptance without a court order; FY2024/25 reporting states "impact on 100,400 companies" and "106,300 companies" for queried/removed false or misleading information, and "10,200 suspicious applications" rejected (annual report and https://www.gov.uk/government/news/progress-made-in-cleaning-up-companies-house-register-as-further-steps-taken-to-tackle-economic-crime).
- **Register data does eventually decay, but only for dissolved entities, and slowly.** Live-company data is not time-limited; dissolved-company data has a 20-year statutory retention horizon (now paused pending review) before archival/destruction.
- **Identity verification is applied retroactively.** "Existing directors must complete verification before filing the first confirmation statement made after 18 November 2025" — an obligatory backfill against an already-onboarded population, not a greenfield-only requirement. https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2025/11/11/how-to-verify-id-with-companies-house.
