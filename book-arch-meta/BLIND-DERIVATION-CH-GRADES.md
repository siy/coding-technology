# Blind Derivation — Companies House: Grades

## The documented architecture (evidence base)

**Search/read path.** `github.com/companieshouse/company-profile-search-consumer` (README): "Creates and updates search indices in the elasticsearch primary index," working with `search.api.ch.gov.uk`, fed by `chs-delta-api` / `company-profile-delta-consumer`. Companies House's own 2019 engineering blog ("Making the best use of cloud capabilities," companieshouse.blog.gov.uk/2019/07/02) describes a cutover where they "stopped Deltas (the process that updates CHS from our internal database processing)" and "Load Elastic Search (the CHS search engine)" — a direct, first-party description of a delta-projection pipeline from the internal system of record into Elasticsearch.

**Legacy core store.** `github.com/companieshouse/oracle-query-api`: "an API service wrapper for CHIPS Oracle DB queries... should be used for all queries from CHS to the CHIPS database." CHIPS (Companies House Information Project System) is the legacy Oracle-backed transactional store; newer services (e.g. `filing-history-data-api`) write to MongoDB collections and communicate via delta/queue messages, not a shared event store.

**Filing corrections.** CH's own RP04 process (summarized by SeedLegals from CH guidance): "The process doesn't remove the original erroneous filing. It will still remain on the register, but will be annotated to indicate that a second filing of the document has been made." The public Filing History API schema literally includes an `annotations` field on each item.

**ECCTA query/reject/annotate.** The Registrar (Annotation, Removal and Disclosure Restrictions) Regulations 2024 (in force 4 March 2024) give Companies House power to "annotate the register where it considers that the registered information is misleading or confusing." Pre-registration queries are rejected with a 14-day resubmission window; post-registration queries can leave the record flagged pending resolution.

**Topology.** `github.com/companieshouse` hosts 670+ repositories. The 2019 blog "Working in digital at Companies House" (companieshouse.blog.gov.uk/2019/03/21): "We'll need to rebuild all of our services onto this new platform, using the microservice pattern we adopted... As fully autonomous teams, the scrums work on delivering Minimum Viable Products." A representative repo sample shows dozens of independently repoed, per-domain API+web pairs (`overseas-entities-api`/`-web`, `psc-verification-api`/`-web`, `limited-partnerships-api`/`-web`, `company-accounts.api`/`-web`, `officer-filing-api`, `confirmation-statement-web`, `strike-off-objections-api`, `accounts-association-api`), each independently deployed via Docker/ECS/Terraform/Concourse.

**Region.** Same 2019 cloud post: launched on AWS eu-west-1 (Dublin) in 2017; moved wholesale to eu-west-2 (London) post-Brexit, extending the Mongo cluster across both regions only as a migration-day mechanism, then "shut down Ireland." "As we know we'll be staying in London for the foreseeable future."

**ROE.** `github.com/companieshouse/overseas-entities-api` and `overseas-entities-web` — separately repoed, separately deployed. Built and launched "in 109 working days" under the Economic Crime (Transparency and Enforcement) Act 2022, distinct from the Companies Act 2006 core register.

**Recovery.** Court-ordered rectification under CA2006 ss.1096–1097 documented at 2–3 months (Hill Dickinson case examples: 2 and 3 months). IDV rollout gated per company's own confirmation-statement anniversary (GOV.UK IDV guidance): "For most PSCs, the 14-day period is linked to the company's annual confirmation statement."

## Prediction grades

1. **HIT.** Elasticsearch-backed `search.api.ch.gov.uk`, populated by delta consumers from the CHIPS/Oracle + MongoDB write side, is explicitly documented — including in CH's own engineering blog's "Deltas" / "Load Elastic Search" language.
2. **HIT.** RP04 guidance and the Filing History API's `annotations` field confirm append-only correction layering; original filings are never mutated in place.
3. **HIT.** `overseas-entities-api`/`-web` are separately repoed and deployed services under a distinct Act (ECTEA 2022 vs Companies Act 2006), built and launched independently. Caveat: no citation confirms physical database separation, only service/repo separation — the prediction's "own data component" wording is satisfied at the service-boundary level, not proven at the storage-engine level.
4. **HIT.** No "event sourcing," "event store," or "CQRS" terminology anywhere in the public companieshouse GitHub corpus; `filing-history-data-api` stores deltas into a MongoDB collection (audit-log-as-data) behind a conventional current-state legacy store (CHIPS/Oracle).
5. **PARTIAL.** The general mechanism is documented: `officer-filing-api` "delivers a filing document (JSON) that is inputted to the CHIPS filing consumer," and a June 2026 incident notice confirms filings are normally queued ("queued documents would be processed in order of submission"). But no citation ties this queue specifically to the December peak — the only December-specific document (the 2018 blog on the 503-filers/28,874-missed figures) is a penalty-deadline warning, not an architecture description. The mechanism claim is well-supported; the December-specific framing is inferred, not attested.
6. **HIT.** The annotation regulations, the `annotations` API field, and the documented 14-day query-then-resubmit-or-remain-rejected flow together constitute exactly the pending/flagged pattern predicted. Caveat: no separately named "case-management" backend repo was found (plausibly internal/private); the pending-state is attested at the legal-process and API-schema level, not via a named service.
7. **HIT.** The 2019 cloud-migration blog is unambiguous: single-region steady state (Dublin, then wholly to London), cross-region Mongo extension used only as a migration-day bridge, then decommissioned. "Staying in London for the foreseeable future" is about as direct a disavowal of active-active multi-region as a primary source gets.
8. **MISS.** This is the derivation's own flagged "most exposed negative claim," and the evidence bears out the exposure: CH's GitHub org has 670+ repos, and CH's own engineering blog explicitly names "the microservice pattern we adopted" with "fully autonomous teams." A representative sample shows dozens of independently deployed, per-domain services. This is not "a smaller number of major subsystems" — it is a genuinely fine-grained microservices decomposition, self-described as such.

## Vector grades

- **Axis 1 (topology) — predicted single deployable + modulith: MISS.** Directly contradicted by the "microservice pattern" self-description and 670+ independently deployed repos. The single largest miss in the vector.
- **Axis 2 (substrate) — predicted event-based (intake/case-mgmt) + direct (reads): HIT.** Filing intake confirmed event/queue-based (CHIPS filing consumer, queued-processing incident language). Reads are synchronous request/response from the caller's perspective (the search API returns an HTTP response directly) — "direct" here describes the client-facing call pattern, which is a different claim from Axis 3's "separated" read model, and the derivation's own consistency-lens section already separately flags the projection-staleness question. Case-management's event-based sub-claim is structurally plausible (state-transition workflow) but not independently documented.
- **Axis 3 (read/write model) — predicted unified (core) + separated (search/bulk): HIT.** Cleanly attested by the Elasticsearch/delta-consumer chain; no evidence of a similar read-scaling split for filing-accept or case-management paths.
- **Axis 4 (state storage) — predicted current-state + audit-log-as-data: HIT.** No event-sourcing/CQRS/event-store language found anywhere; filing history stored as an appended MongoDB collection behind a conventional legacy store.
- **Axis 5 (persistence) — predicted single shared (core) + per-component (ROE): PARTIAL.** ROE's per-component isolation is a clean HIT (separate repos/deployment). "Single shared" for the core overstates the documented picture, though: the record shows a legacy CHIPS/Oracle backbone plus several service-owned MongoDB collections and an Elasticsearch index — closer to database-per-service atop one shared legacy system of record than to one literal shared store. The "not distributed-shared / no multi-region" part is solidly right; the "single" part is finer-grained than documented.
- **Axis 6 (recovery) — predicted mixed design-out/FER/BER: HIT.** RP04 matches design-out; ECCTA's 14-day query window and anniversary-gated IDV backfill match FER; court-ordered rectification (2–3 months, documented residual reliance risk) matches BER precisely.

## Read-path verdict

Public register reads are directly attested — not merely inferred — to be served by an Elasticsearch-backed search index (`search.api.ch.gov.uk`) that is kept current through a delta/event pipeline from the write-side stores (legacy CHIPS/Oracle plus newer service-owned MongoDB collections), rather than by querying the primary transactional store per search; Companies House's own engineering blog names this pipeline ("Deltas," "Load Elastic Search") in describing a 2019 infrastructure migration.

## Misses and what they teach

**Prediction 8 / Axis 1 (topology) — the real miss.** The derivation used F21 ("headcount presses cadence, not topology") plus three inert UNKNOWNs to argue against decomposition, and explicitly flagged this as its riskiest claim in advance — correctly. The self-awareness was earned: this is exactly where it broke. But the failure isn't obviously a logic error; it looks more like an answer-sheet gap. Nothing in the pre-registered pressure pass cites an explicit "we build microservices" statement — had that fact been available to the method (it exists, in CH's own 2019 blog), the method's own citation discipline would have forced a services-leaning topology call rather than the modulith default. The quarantine log even names and discounts the "known public digital-transformation program" awareness that, in this case, would have pointed the right way. This is a case where caution about an unforced instinct was sound methodology applied to an incomplete evidence set, not a wrong method.

**Axis 5 "single shared" — a vocabulary gap, not a direction error.** The core-register persistence is real but not accurately captured by "single shared" — it's closer to a database-per-service pattern sitting on a shared legacy backbone, an intermediate shape the six-axis vocabulary doesn't cleanly name between "single shared" and "distributed-shared."

**Prediction 5's December-specific framing — an unforced specificity the record doesn't settle.** The underlying mechanism (queued filing ingestion) is solidly attested; tying it explicitly to the December peak was a reasonable bridge the deriver built from two separately-true facts (queue exists; December is the volume peak) that the public record never states together.

## Scoreboard

**Predictions:** 6 HIT, 1 PARTIAL, 1 MISS, 0 UNGRADEABLE (of 8).
**Vector axes:** 4 HIT, 1 PARTIAL, 1 MISS, 0 UNGRADEABLE (of 6).
**Combined:** 10 HIT, 2 PARTIAL, 2 MISS, 0 UNGRADEABLE (of 14).

Overall: the derivation's consistency-lens reasoning — everything downstream of "what guarantee does this operation need, and what's the cheapest mechanism that earns it" — scored close to perfectly, including several unexpectedly precise documentary confirmations (the literal `annotations` API field, the literal "Deltas"/"Load Elastic Search" migration language, the CHIPS-filing-consumer queue). The one clear, substantial failure is topology: Companies House is extensively and self-describedly decomposed into microservices, which the derivation predicted against on its own most self-flagged-as-risky claim. That validates the method's ability to locate its own weak point in advance, while showing "headcount doesn't press topology" is not a safe universal default — real organizations sometimes choose fine-grained decomposition as an explicit strategic pattern, independent of (if correlated with) the growth pressure the ledger currently models.
